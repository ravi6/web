🫓 Inside the RotiMaker: Teardown & Blank-Slate Hijack

Zero hardware modifications. One erased factory microcontroller. Here is what happens when you crack open an automated roti maker, reverse-engineer its circuit board, and teach it to cook from scratch with custom firmware.

⚠️ High Voltage & Thermal Notice: This appliance operates on lethal mains AC voltage and high-wattage heating plates. All testing shown below was performed on the bench with safety isolation and dummy loads.
01 · Cracking the Shell

A deceptively clean Machine

But does not function, just makes a beep with no display

01 · Cracking the Shell

Meeting the Nervous System

Popping off the outer housing reveals a surprisingly dense green control board connected to a rainbow of labeled wire harnesses (PM4, PM5, and friends). Not a single wire cut—just pure snooping.

02 · The Brain

A PIC32 Waiting for a Blank Slate

At the heart of the board sits a 32-bit Microchip PIC32MX470F512L microcontroller. Step one of the hijack: wiping the factory firmware completely clean so we can write our own from line zero.

03 · Precision Sensing

How It Weighs Flour & Reads Heat

The board is equipped with Texas Instruments ADS1148 16-bit precision ADC—the chip responsible for turning tiny thermocouple and load-cell signals into actual temperatures and dough weights. This second image is the replaced chip soon after I drag soldered replacement chip when I accidentally cooked it with 12V trying to configure the chip with stimulated TC signals.

04 · Underneath the Hood

Load Cells, Gear Trains & Plumbing

Peeking beneath the upper deck reveals the aluminum strain-gauge load cell (which weighs the flour and water), gear drives, and silicone tubes that meter out water and oil.

05 · The Muscle

Serious Torque for Squashing Dough

Pulling the main DC gearmotor off the cast-aluminum press frame. Flattening a dough ball into a thin roti takes a serious gearbox—definitely not a toy motor. Had to dismantle the motorgear train to release jammed press screw when learning to drive the motor in a controlled manner

06 · First Motion Test (Video)

Elevator Going Up: Mixing Cup Actuator

First mechanical victory under custom code—commanding the mixing cup lift mechanism to travel smoothly up and down on cue. the cup stays up but the round bottom moves up and once the flour with water tuns to a ball, it descends and the dough ball is pushed over the platten with swinging arm.

07 · Dough Choreography (Video)

Spinning the Flour Mixer & Ball Roller

The center hub drives the paddle (removed here) which meters the flour into bottom bowl (removed here) and kneadng arm is driven by another motor off center to the hub. The spinning ribbed roller job is still not clear. The kneader arm is attached to top cup. Getting the timing right between the kneading blade and the dough-ball roller—all driven directly by our clean-slate PIC32 firmware is tricky.

08 · Where the Heat Lives

Cast-Aluminum Platens & High-Temp Sleeves

Top and bottom heating platens view. The top section rear half is driven down by the press to roll the roti flat, the front half just rides on the edge of rear half. This allows for the roti to be heated from above in the final stage to puff it up, before the pusher pushes it out of roti machine. Notice the black stip at the rear of the platens is the roti pushing boom in final stages

Embedded tubular heating element and fiberglass-sleeved sensor wires. This is the part you really don't want a software bug leaving stuck ON. This picture is from web trawls

A rear view of the roti press platen with heater element casing, and the bottom sliding frame (roti pusher)pushes the roti towards the exit. The encoder wheel is for the roti pusher position control.

09 · Safe Heater Testing (Video)

Dummy-Load Testing with a Light Globe

Before letting our custom code energize real 240V heating plates, we wired up an incandescent light globe as a visual dummy load while verifying live temperature sensor feedback.

The open machine hooked up to the Mac over serial (picocom), streaming real-time temperature readings across all four heater zones from our custom firmware.
